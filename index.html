Looking Back

Course Reflection

Three years of growth, challenges, and breakthroughs.

Sophomore & Junior Year

Sophomore year taught me the fundamentals — Bootstrap layouts, responsive design, and how to debug patiently. Junior year is where things accelerated: JavaScript opened up games, generators, and real interactivity. I learned to think in functions, manage state, and trace logic errors instead of just guessing.

Senior Year & Takeaways

Senior year meant raising the bar — jQuery, AJAX, a university partnership, and a real client project. Working with Z Best Goodies taught me that communication matters as much as code. The best way to learn is to build things just out of reach. The web never stops changing; the skill is knowing how to figure it out.

Looking Ahead

Future Plans

Where I want to go from here.

College

I'll be attending The College of New Jersey (TCNJ) to study Mechanical Engineering. I'm excited to apply the same problem-solving mindset I built in web design to a new field — engineering is all about breaking complex problems into systems that actually work, which is exactly what good code does too.

Keeping Web Skills Sharp

Even as I shift focus to engineering, I plan to keep building on the front-end skills I've spent three years developing. Web design is something I genuinely enjoy, and I see it as a useful edge in engineering — whether it's building tools, visualizations, or personal projects on the side.
